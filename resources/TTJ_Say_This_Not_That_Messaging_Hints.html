TechTonic Justice · Messaging guidance

Say This, Not That

Concrete language examples and practical hints for communicating the real-world harms of AI.

Start with the person affected. Name the institution choosing to use AI, explain the action it takes, and show the consequence. These examples are writing guidance, not individually tested polling messages.

Health care

Say this
Your doctor says you need the treatment. Your insurer’s AI says you cannot have it.
Not that
AI is affecting access to health care.

Public benefits

Say this
The Social Security Administration uses an AI system that falsely flags you for fraud which immediately cuts off your benefits. Nobody can explain why and you can’t reach a human for help.
Not that
Automated systems can affect access to public benefits.

Housing

Say this
Landlords are using AI screening systems that can falsely flag people for evictions or mix them up with someone who has the same name. The applications are denied and the renters have no idea why.
Not that
AI can create risks for tenants.

Other messaging hints

Person + decision-maker + action + consequence. Show who may be harmed, who chose to use the system, what the system was used to do, and what someone could lose.

Make responsibility visible. Say who should explain the decision and who has the authority to fix a mistake.

Use precise examples. Explain the relevant harm in plain language and avoid unsupported claims about a particular case.

Connect concern to protection. Name concrete responses: preventing harmful uses, maintaining meaningful human access, and repairing harm when it occurs.
